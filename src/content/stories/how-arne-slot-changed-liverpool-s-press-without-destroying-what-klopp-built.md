Anfield, 1 December 2024. Twelve minutes gone, no score, and Manchester City are pressing Liverpool out of a 4-4-2. If you want to understand Arne Slot's Liverpool tactics, this is the passage to rewind. Most people remember it for a different reason.

Alexis Mac Allister drops out of Liverpool's 4-3-3 and stands next to Ryan Gravenberch. City's two central midfielders go with him, which leaves nobody behind them. Trent Alexander-Arnold looks up and hits the diagonal to Mohamed Salah on the right. Salah slides it low across Stefan Ortega's six-yard box, and Cody Gakpo, who has come in on a diagonal run inside City's full-back, turns it in at the far post.

Salah added a penalty in the 78th minute after Ortega brought down Luis Díaz. Liverpool had 19 shots to City's seven, the most any opponent managed against Pep Guardiola's side all season, according to [Coaches' Voice](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/cv/liverpool-2-manchester-city-0-tactical-analysis/). As [Sky Sports](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/11661/13262408/liverpool-2-0-man-city-cody-gakpo-and-mohamed-salah-secure-victory-as-crisis-deepens-for-pep-guardiola) pointed out, it was also City's fourth straight league defeat, their first run like that since 2008.

The goal lives on as a Salah highlight. The more interesting question is who was pressing whom. City went high, Liverpool played through them, and the space City left behind decided the game. Three years earlier you could have written that sentence about Jürgen Klopp's side doing it to somebody else.

## How did Arne Slot change Liverpool's press?

Slot replaced Klopp's front-three press with a selective one. Liverpool sat in a mid-block and jumped only when a trigger appeared, so they pressed less often and chose when they did.

Under Klopp the whole front three pressed, and the gegenpress (winning the ball back straight after losing it) was designed to be relentless. Slot changed the shape. [Coaches' Voice's breakdown of his system](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/cv/arne-slot-liverpool-tactics/) shows Dominik Szoboszlai stepping out of midfield to press alongside the centre-forward. That made the 4-3-3 a 4-4-2, or a 4-2-4 when the wingers pushed on. Gakpo and Salah tucked in to block passes to the outside shoulder of the front two, and Mac Allister and Gravenberch held behind as a two-man pivot.

The triggers were specific. A pass back to the goalkeeper. A centre-back receiving side-on. An opposition No 8 dropping in to collect. If none of those came, Liverpool held the block and waited.

The numbers moved straight away. [Opta Analyst](https://theanalyst.com/2024/10/liverpool-analysing-arne-slot-start-jurgen-klopp) had Liverpool's PPDA at 8.9 in Klopp's final season and 10.4 after Slot's first seven league games. PPDA stands for passes per defensive action. It counts how many passes an opponent gets before Liverpool make a tackle, interception or foul, so a lower number means a more aggressive press. High turnovers fell from 10.3 a game to 8.7. Final-third regains dropped to 4.3 a game, fewer than in any full season under Klopp.

Curtis Jones explained it better than a diagram could: "In the past, it was a rush to get the ball back, a bit too direct, up and down, up and down. Now we want to have all the ball and completely kill a team."

## Did Slot's Liverpool stop gegenpressing?

No. They pressed less, got more out of each press and won the 2024-25 title doing it. Few teams in the league turned high turnovers into shots as often.

[Opta Analyst's review of 2024-25 playing styles](https://theanalyst.com/articles/analysing-premier-league-playing-styles-2024-25) found that 21.1% of Liverpool's high turnovers ended in a shot, one of the top three rates in the Premier League. Gravenberch, mostly a substitute under Klopp, led the squad with 44 high turnovers. Eight of them led directly to a shot.

Opta also credited Liverpool with 31 goals from transitions, at least 10 more than any other top-flight side. So the gegenpress was still there. Slot had just made it pickier about when to go.

The first season ended on 84 points, with 86 goals scored, 41 conceded and a 10-point gap to Arsenal ([FBref](https://fbref.com/en/squads/822bd0ba/2024-2025/Liverpool-Stats)). Gravenberch made the whole thing work. He recovered ground so quickly that a two-man pivot could cover a lot of pitch. That let Szoboszlai step into the first line, and that in turn freed Díaz to drop short between the lines as a sort of false 9 in games like the City win.

It held up in Europe as well. On 27 November 2024, four days before City came to Anfield, Liverpool beat Real Madrid 2-0. They defended in a narrow 4-1-4-1 that [Coaches' Voice](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/cv/liverpool-real-madrid-2024-tactics/) described as shuffling across as one unit, then broke into the space behind Madrid's advanced full-backs. Mac Allister scored in the 52nd minute, Gakpo in the 76th, and in between Caoimhín Kelleher saved a penalty from Kylian Mbappé.

## Why did Liverpool collapse under Slot in 2025-26?

The popular explanation is that the softer press finally caught up with Slot. I don't think the data supports it. Opponents stopped giving Liverpool anything to press, mostly by kicking the ball over them, and the real damage happened behind the press.

The case against Slot is worth laying out properly. [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/soccer/staggering-liverpool-statistic-exposes-arne-slot-biggest-flaw) called it his "biggest flaw". Liverpool went from 177 presses per match in 2024-25 to 141 early in 2025-26. That was a 20.4% drop and the lowest figure in the league. A [widely shared analysis](https://ca.sports.yahoo.com/news/liverpool-lost-chaotic-edge-made-081733135.html) asked whether Liverpool had "lost the chaotic edge" that made them so feared. They finished fifth on 60 points, with 12 defeats and 53 goals conceded, and [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/soccer/story/_/id/48918413/arne-slot-leaves-liverpool-immediate-effect) reported that Slot was removed on 30 May 2026. The version that stuck was simple: the press went soft and the team fell apart.

That gets cause and effect the wrong way round.

Start with the title season. By the usual measures the press was already "soft" then. PPDA had gone up, high turnovers had gone down, and Liverpool still won the league by 10 points. If pressing less caused the 2025-26 collapse, it should have hurt them a year earlier.

Then look at who won 2025-26. SI's own figures had Arsenal second-lowest in the league on 147 presses per match, and [Arsenal won the title](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4662306/arsenal-win-2025-26-premier-league-title-ending-22-year-wait-to-be-crowned-champions-again) with 85 points. So the bottom two sides for press volume were the champions and the team that finished fifth. Whatever split those two clubs, it wasn't how many presses they made.

And much of that 20.4% drop wasn't Liverpool's choice. After nine games, [Opta Analyst](https://theanalyst.com/articles/liverpool-long-balls-premier-league-stats) found Liverpool had faced 571 long balls, at least 46 more than anyone else. That was 20.5% of all passes played against them, the highest share in the division. The three biggest long-ball totals of the season all came against Liverpool: 75 by Manchester United, 71 by Crystal Palace and 70 by Bournemouth. The [Premier League's own analysis](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4449990/analysis-why-long-balls-are-proving-so-effective-against-liverpool) showed opponents' open-play passes against Liverpool had fallen by 11.9%.

It's hard to press a ball that's 20 metres above your head. Liverpool's press numbers fell because opponents stopped playing the passes that trigger a press.

## How did opponents exploit Slot's Liverpool?

Opponents found two ways. Most went long to bypass the press and then fought for the second ball. The better possession sides packed central midfield and got an extra man past the narrow mid-block. Both targeted the space behind Liverpool's pivot.

The strongest evidence is the long-ball split. The Premier League's analysis covered Slot's first 49 league games and found 17 in which long balls made up 20% or more of the opponent's passes. Liverpool won 41% of those games and 75% of the other 32. When opponents passed into the press, it worked. When they went over it, Liverpool didn't have a plan B.

Manchester United's 2-1 win at Anfield on 19 October 2025 was an early example. Ruben Amorim set up a 5-4-1 low block and went long whenever he could. United scored after 62 seconds. A long ball looped up, Virgil van Dijk's challenge on Bryan Mbeumo caught Mac Allister, Bruno Fernandes picked up the loose ball and found Amad Diallo, and Amad set up Mbeumo. Gakpo hit the post three times before Harry Maguire headed a late winner. Slot got mocked afterwards for complaining about "so many long balls", but he was right about what had happened.

The second problem was built into the system. Slot's full-backs and midfielders pushed up so willingly that, according to [Football Bunseki's analysis](https://footballbunsekicom.com/team-analysis/how-to-beat-liverpool-arne-slots-liverpool-tactical-analysis-25-26/), defending counter-attacks came down largely to how good the centre-backs were individually. Van Dijk kept winning his duels, 46 of 59 aerials (78%) at the point the Premier League ran its numbers. But if the system needs one centre-back to win every header, it breaks the first time he loses one.

City showed the possession version of the same problem in their 3-0 win on 9 November 2025. [Coaches' Voice](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/cv/manchester-city-liverpool-tactics-november-2025/) described Jérémy Doku and Rayan Cherki drifting in off the wings to join Phil Foden. Nico O'Reilly and Matheus Nunes provided the width, and Bernardo Silva dropped in next to Nico González as a double pivot. That put five City players in central midfield. Liverpool narrowed their 4-2-3-1, with Salah and Florian Wirtz tucked in, and City simply went around them. They played short passes at a slow tempo to draw Liverpool in, then hit a quick ball into the middle and out to the wing.

It's tempting to read that game as proof Liverpool needed Klopp's intensity back. I'd argue it shows the reverse. City had a spare man in midfield all afternoon. An all-out, man-for-man press would only have left that player freer, and Van Dijk and Ibrahima Konaté would have been facing Doku one-on-one in open grass. Liverpool were short of bodies in central midfield, and pressing harder wouldn't have fixed that.

## What did Slot keep from Klopp, and what does it mean for Iraola?

Slot kept what mattered most from Klopp's Liverpool: win the ball back and attack immediately, as fast as possible. He changed how often they chased and where they did it. His successor inherits that argument, and it hasn't been settled.

Go back to the Gakpo goal. Klopp's best teams lived in the moment just after the ball changes hands, when you can win it back or attack before the opponent is set. Slot never gave that up. The 31 transition goals in 2024-25 show Klopp's ideas were still there. Slot just used them more selectively.

What Slot added was restraint, and restraint has costs he never fully covered. A team that picks its moments to press has to stay compact from front to back when it isn't pressing. It needs players who win the second ball after a long pass, and it needs cover for the space its full-backs vacate. The title side had those things in Alexander-Arnold's range of passing, Gravenberch's recovery runs and a settled back line. In 2025-26, [set-piece problems](https://theanalyst.com/articles/liverpool-set-piece-struggles-premier-league) and a string of defensive injuries took them away, and opposition coaches noticed.

That's why press volume misleads. It reflects how many chances the opponent gives you as much as how hard your players are willing to run. Liverpool pressed 177 times a match when teams tried to play through them and 141 times when teams went long. The players didn't get lazier between those two numbers.

Andoni Iraola was appointed on 4 June 2026 on a two-year contract. His Bournemouth side was one of the league's most aggressive pressing teams by [Opta Analyst's](https://theanalyst.com/articles/andoni-iraola-tactics-premier-league-liverpool-bournemouth) measures. The early 2026-27 results include draws with Newcastle, Nottingham Forest and Fulham. The appointment suggests the club thinks intensity was what went missing. I read the evidence differently. Slot's press worked until opponents found a way around it, and nobody rebuilt what sat behind it.

Every side that goes long against Iraola's Liverpool this season will test that. If they keep winning second balls and the long-ball split doesn't change, the press was never the problem.
